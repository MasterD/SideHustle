SRT or VTT? A practical subtitle export checklist

Already have subtitles, but need a different file format, a transcript or a timing fix? Here is a short checklist for the final export step.

1. Keep an original copy

Work on a duplicate of your existing SRT or WebVTT file. Decide whether the destination needs SRT, VTT, plain text, a timed transcript or a CSV cue sheet. A transcript is useful for reading or notes; a subtitle file keeps the cue timings needed for playback.

2. Check what conversion will preserve

For example, an SRT cue can look like this:

1
00:00:01,000 --> 00:00:03,000
Welcome to the lesson.

In the kit's simple WebVTT export, the same cue becomes:

WEBVTT

00:00:01.000 --> 00:00:03.000
Welcome to the lesson.

Caption Cleanup Kit keeps the cue text and timings, but omits WebVTT notes, styles, regions, cue identifiers and positioning settings. Keep the source if you need those features.

3. Fix a constant timing offset carefully

If every cue starts one second late, a minus-one-second offset may help. If the mismatch grows as the video plays, a single offset will not fix it. Check the start, middle and end against your media. The kit rejects a shift that would put a cue before zero.

4. Review the text and timings

Tidy extra spaces and wrap lines where appropriate. Check overlap, cue order, line length and reading speed. The kit flags possible issues for editing; it does not check the words against your media or certify accessibility.

5. Review the exported batch

Use the review report, spot-check the converted files and test the result in your destination player or editor. Keep your source files alongside the exports.
