## Decision

Start with one sole-proprietor service offer under the approved working name **QuietBatch**, operated by **Theunis Christian Botha**, contact **quietbatchreports@gmail.com**. The owner approved the direction and confirms outside-hours work requires no employer approval. Serve customers remotely across South Africa. Sell a repeatable, fixed-scope report built from customer CSV exports. Deliver a local script and a run guide. The customer uses their existing computer; no cloud service or AI API is necessary.

SARS describes a sole proprietorship as belonging to the individual, with business income reported in the owner's own tax return. A trade name does not create a separate legal entity, and the owner has personal liability. This is the owner's approved starting structure; creating multiple companies would add obligations without proving demand. [SARS sole proprietorship guidance](https://www.sars.gov.za/businesses-and-employers/small-businesses-taxpayers/starting-a-business-and-tax/sole-proprietorship/)

The first customer hypothesis is a small South African design, marketing or other service agency whose owner spends time consolidating sales exports and outstanding invoices. We must validate this problem and willingness to pay. Upwork's 2026 report lists scripting/automation and data analytics among sought-after skills; that is a global platform signal, not evidence that a local customer will buy this offer. [Upwork research](https://www.upwork.com/press/releases/upworks-in-demand-skills-2026-demand-for-top-ai-skills-more-than-doubles-as-ai-is-embedded-into-everyday-work)

## Cash and workload

Approved upfront spending: **R0**. No advertisements, domain purchases, subscriptions, inventory orders, paid leads or purchased proposal credits. Use existing computer, internet and software access. Incremental electricity, banking charges, subscription overages and owner time are real costs if incurred; record them rather than describing the business as literally costless. Income tax remains payable when due.

Customers pay by EFT to the owner's chosen FNB personal account. Do not assume its specific product permits the intended business use or is fee-free; the owner checks its terms first. No automatic bank access is needed. Confirm cleared funds against an actual statement, not a payment screenshot. Quote 50% on agreement and 50% after acceptance; retain enough of a deposit to meet delivery and any refund obligation. Customer funds are not automatically distributable profit.

Allow only one active pilot. Initial owner workload over two weeks: up to 90 minutes of discovery/sales, 120 minutes of sample and delivery review, 30 minutes of records, and 120 minutes of buffer. Actual time will be measured. Assistant-generated code can reduce repetitive production work, but the owner still checks outputs, handles customers and accepts responsibility. If work exceeds the three-hour weekly limit, reduce scope or extend the delivery slot before agreeing it.

One paid R3,500 pilot is the initial validation goal, not a forecast. Two fully paid pilots in a month would mean R7,000 gross receipts before fees, refunds, income tax and owner time. The arithmetic assumes two customers actually buy and accept delivery; current receipts are R0. Do not plan living expenses around this scenario.

## First 30 days after approval

1. Preparation before trading: name, email and outside-hours work are owner-confirmed. Complete the seller disclosures, privacy/information-officer setup, bank-account checks and provisional-tax review. The owner reviews the synthetic demo and final customer page. Then identify five appropriate people from the owner's existing professional network and obtain permission to discuss the problem. Drafting is permitted; sending messages requires explicit approval of recipients and text.
2. Week 2: conduct up to three short discovery conversations. Ask how often the report is produced, which exports exist, how mistakes are checked and whether a R3,500 pilot would be useful. Use anonymised samples. Send a quote only after the owner approves it.
3. Week 3: deliver one accepted pilot with written scope, customer-approved data and a reproducible acceptance check. Count revenue when the real payment is recorded; pipeline value remains separate.
4. Week 4: reconcile payments, costs, delivery time and feedback. Ask permission for any testimonial or case study. Reuse only generic code we have rights to use. Continue if the customer accepts the report and the workload fits; otherwise revise scope or price.

Decision checkpoint: after five suitable conversations, if nobody wants a paid pilot, change the problem or offer. Do not buy advertising to compensate. After three pilots, consider a reusable kit only if the common workflow and distribution are clear. These are proposed experiment rules, not researched conversion rates.

## Sourcing and platform decisions

There is no manufacturing requirement for this initial service: the deliverable is code we create, using customer-provided exports and licensed standard-library tools. Keep third-party code licence notices and check rights before reselling anything. Never reuse employer code, employer time, proprietary datasets or customer data from another engagement.

Physical products can be sourced later, but are not compatible with a guaranteed R0 cash exposure today. Printful is an example of outsourced production with order-level product, fulfilment and shipping charges; the merchant is billed separately from the customer's store payment. Payout timing can create a cash gap. Its availability is not proof of profitable South African fulfilment. [Printful pricing](https://www.printful.com/pricing), [billing](https://help.printful.com/hc/en-us/articles/50264607936785-How-does-the-Printful-billing-system-work)

Digital marketplaces also have costs. Gumroad's help page lists direct-sale fees plus processing charges; merchant sales-tax handling does not remove the owner's South African income-tax duties. Upwork proposals can consume paid Connects. Neither platform is authorised for use or paid acquisition here. Recheck eligibility, payout timing, currency conversion and the exact fee schedule before any later launch. [Gumroad fees](https://gumroad.com/help/article/66-gumroads-fees), [Upwork Connects](https://support.upwork.com/hc/en-us/articles/211062898-Understanding-and-using-Connects)

For any future physical product, obtain the supplier's legal identity, wholesale invoice, price including applicable VAT, delivery cost and location, fulfilment timeframe, tracking, stock accuracy, defects/returns process and permission to use product images. Calculate margin after payment fees, shipping, imports, returns and tax. Require customer funds to clear before a supplier charge, with a refund reserve; no store opens until that cashflow is workable and the owner approves it.
