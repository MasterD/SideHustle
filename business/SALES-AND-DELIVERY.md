## Customer discovery

Best initial fit: a small service-agency owner with an existing weekly manual report, CSV exports and authority to approve a pilot. Avoid new system rollouts, bank integrations, real-time financial advice, medical records, payroll and workflows requiring write access in the first pilot.

Ask: What report do you produce each week? Which exports go into it? How long does it take? Which totals must match? Would you prefer a reusable local report over paying for another monthly application? Is the proposed R3,500 pilot worth testing? Do not claim a quantified saving until their process has been measured.

## Scope before acceptance

- Confirm ZAR amounts and whether input columns include VAT; define sales, payments, invoice balances and overdue dates distinctly.
- Obtain only permitted and necessary data. Use aliases and synthetic/anonymised inputs during discovery.
- Agree export headers and maximum expected rows, positive/negative value handling, credit notes, date format and the exact report period.
- Write five or fewer measures with expected results on the agreed sample. Confirm who verifies the underlying export.
- Confirm the customer's existing Python-compatible environment or quote a different package only when there is a working free delivery path.
- Confirm one delivery slot fits the owner's time limit. Do not accept a second active pilot.

## Delivery and acceptance

Use the sample script as an example, not a universal accounting engine. Adapt the customer's schemas, validate input data and fail clearly on unexpected files. Check boundary dates, duplicate transactions, refunds/credits if in scope, VAT definitions and totals against the approved sample. Deliver the script, outputs, run guide, required licence notices and acceptance checklist. Avoid any scheduled execution until the customer approves its timing and access.

The customer verifies input truth and agreed totals. Correct defects in the agreed scope; quote requested new functionality separately. Keep dated acceptance and invoice evidence. Remove working customer files as agreed, while retaining the required business records and minimum contractual evidence.

## Weekly owner review — proposed 15-minute routine

Reconcile payments and costs; look for overdue customer acceptance or refund obligations; back up records; review tax/cost alerts; pick one next sales action. This is a routine proposal, not a scheduled automation. The monitor only refreshes when running and cannot observe a bank account without a later approved integration.
